Robots and the FCC’s July 2026 notice.

On 28 July 2026 the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) added foreign-produced “advanced robotic devices” to its Covered List. If you own, or are thinking of buying, a robot vacuum, a robot lawn mower, a robot dog or a humanoid, this page says what the notice covers, what it does, and what it does not say. Every quotation is from the FCC’s own documents, with its page. Read on 13 November 2026.

This is our reading of the text. It is not legal advice and not a ruling on any model. We name no model on this page, because we could not read the FCC’s authorization records ourselves. The same notice also covers power inverters; this page does not.

What the notice covers

The Covered List now includes “Foreign-produced advanced robotic devices, except advanced robotic devices which have been granted a Conditional Approval by DoW.” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 3)

It goes by where a device is made, not by who makes it: “this newly-covered equipment is identified by place of production, not by entity.” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 4) No company is named.

The notice takes its definition from a national security determination attached as Appendix C. An advanced robotic device is “A mechanical mobile device, including autonomous mobile robots, humanoid robots, and quadrupeds,” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14) that meets every one of these:

  1. “Is capable of locomotion, obstacle avoidance, navigation, or movement on the ground;” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  2. “Operates at a distance from a human operator or supervisor based on commands or in response to sensor data or any combination thereof; and” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  3. “The combined weight of the device and, if applicable, ground station or docking station is over 4.4lbs; and” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  4. it contains all three of: “a sensor capable of perceiving its environment;” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14) “a component that is capable of providing network connectivity (wired or wireless, including Bluetooth/WiFi, cellular, or satellite) with connection speeds of at least 200 kbps in either direction; and” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14) and “software running either locally or remotely, including firmware and AI or machine-learning model weights, that controls the robot's autonomous navigation or movement perception, data collection, or remote command-and-control; and” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)

It does not include:

  • “A ‘connected vehicle,’ as that term is defined in 15 CFR § 791.301, but including a connected vehicle of any gross weight;” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  • “A vehicle operated only on a rail line;” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  • “An ‘uncrewed aircraft’ or ‘uncrewed aircraft system,’ as defined in 47 CFR § 7 88.5;” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  • “An unmanned underwater vehicle that is able to operate without a human occupant;” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14)
  • “Items that are classified as devices under section 513 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (21 U.S.C. §360c), including surgical instruments,” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 14) “medical and surgical robotic systems, external limb prostheses and their components, and ambulatory and mobility assistive devices (such as canes, crutches, walkers, and wheelchairs), whether or not powered,” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 15)
  • “A fixed, stationary, non-mobile robot, including articulating, parallel/delta, Cartesian/gantry, or Selective Compliance Assembly, or Articulated, Robot Arm (SCARA) robots intended for industrial or medical use.” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 15)

Foreign-produced, in the determination, “refers to any article that would not qualify as a ‘domestic end product,’ as that term is defined in 48 CFR § 25.101(a).” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 15)

In plain words, our reading: a robot made outside the US is covered if it moves on the ground, works at a distance from the person running it, on commands or on what its sensors tell it, weighs more than 4.4 pounds together with its dock, and has a sensor, a network link of at least 200 kbps (Wi-Fi or Bluetooth counts) and software that steers it. A robot vacuum with a dock, a robot lawn mower, a robot dog or a humanoid can meet every part. Whether a given model does depends on its weight, its network link and where it is made. A small desktop robot that weighs 4.4 pounds or less with its dock does not meet the weight part. A robot with no network link at all does not meet the network part. The “connected vehicle” exclusion points to a definition of vehicles “manufactured primarily for use on public streets, roads, and highways” (15 CFR 791.301), which a robot mower is not.

What it does

  • New authorizations stop. Under the FCC’s rules, “once added to the Covered List, ‘covered’ equipment is prohibited from receiving equipment authorizations.” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 3)
  • Updates continue for earlier models. “All advanced robotic devices and power inverters authorized for use in the United States may continue to receive software and firmware updates that mitigate harm to U.S. consumers at least until January 1, 2029.” (FCC DA 26-789, PDF p. 1) The waiver applies to devices “authorized prior to the July 28, 2026, Covered List additions.” (FCC DA 26-789, PDF p. 2)
  • A maker can ask for an exemption. Makers that produce these devices abroad “can request an evaluation as to whether their devices do not pose unacceptable risks to national security and receive Conditional Approvals that would exempt the devices from the Covered List.” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 3) “DoW may evaluate such submissions for advanced robotic devices.” (FCC DA 26-786, PDF p. 3)

What it does not say

  • Whether a model authorized before 28 July 2026 may keep being sold. Neither notice says it may, and neither says it may not.
  • Which models are affected. The notice names no company and no model; it goes by where a device is made.
  • Anything about robots people already own, apart from the software updates above.

Looking up a model. A device with a radio sold in the US usually carries an FCC ID on its label, in its manual or in its settings. The FCC’s equipment authorization search at fcc.gov/oet/ea/fccid shows the grants for an FCC ID and their dates. The FCC’s site refuses automated readers such as ours, so we have not checked any model there, and the notices do not say what a grant date means for a sale.
